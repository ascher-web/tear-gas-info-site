Community Research

Minneapolis 2025–2026: Documenting the Health Impacts

Were you in Minneapolis during ICE and other federal enforcement operations between late December 2025 and March 2026? Your experience matters.

Take the community health survey

The survey is hosted on the University of Minnesota's REDCap research system.

Open the survey →

Who should take the survey?

If you lived in, worked in, or visited Minneapolis during this time, you may be able to take part if you:

  • Were exposed to tear gas, pepper spray, pepper balls, or other less-lethal weapons
  • Had breathing trouble, eye irritation, skin injuries, menstrual changes, or other symptoms after exposure
  • Were injured during an encounter involving federal agents
  • Saw someone else get hurt or exposed to chemical irritants
  • Felt stressed, anxious, or upset after seeing force being used
  • Were a nearby resident, worker, pedestrian, or bystander

You don't have to have been directly exposed or injured. Experiences from witnesses and community members are important too.

Why we're collecting this information

As the rest of this site shows, the long-term health effects of chemical irritants and other less-lethal weapons are not well studied, especially in everyday civilians. Collecting firsthand accounts can help researchers spot patterns in symptoms, understand what the community went through, and show where more medical research is needed.

See Reproductive Health, Respiratory Health, and Long-Term Effects for what is already known.

Before you start

Taking the survey is voluntary. Please read the consent and privacy information on the survey itself to learn how your answers will be used and protected before you submit.

If you are having symptoms now, get medical care first. See Medical Care.
